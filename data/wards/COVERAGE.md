# Ward polygons outside Karnataka: inventory and coverage

Generated by `node infra/aws-central/tools/india-ward-coverage.mjs` from road-notice-manifest-v1.36.json (notices retrieved 2026-10-05), data/wards/index.json, data/wards/india-localities.json, data/wards/handread.json and, for section 11, data/wards/runtime.json and data/wards/runtime-handread.json. Network facts (a source's status, count and licence text) are as read on 7 Oct 2026. Do not edit by hand: change the tool or the data and run it again.

## 1. The short version

- The app holds 7,173 State road notices from 30 States and UTs. 2,605 (36%) were tendered by a body the portal names as urban; the rest are public works divisions, zilla parishads, grama panchayats and rural works agencies.
- 3,033 titles (42%) carry a ward marker, nearly always a number ("Ward No. 27", "Zone-6 Ward-19", "Div-128"). Outside Bengaluru the ward number, read together with the body, is what a title offers; the service's name rule finds a ward's name in 51 of the 304 notices of the bodies snapshotted here.
- Ward polygons that anyone may copy exist for 26 bodies, in 28 files (2,299 wards, 2,599,190 bytes). Those bodies, with every Delhi and Chandigarh notice counted as the city's, account for 304 of the 7,173 notices (4.2%). Ghaziabad alone tendered 321 and has no polygons anyone may copy.
- Having polygons is not the same as being able to use them. A person read 142 ward and notice pairs in the six bodies with the most notices: see section 7. In short: Ahmedabad works by name (30 of 30 right), Bhopal works by number once the zone number stops being read as a ward (12 of 30 wrong, all for that one reason), and the Kanpur, Jaipur and Hyderabad files are numbered for another delimitation than their 2026 tenders (20 of 30, 14 of 15 and 15 of 22 pairs wrong; in Hyderabad 10 of those because a circle number was read as a ward).
- The service's ward number parser was written for Bengaluru titles. On 61 titles elsewhere it returns a zone or circle number as a ward ("WARD 47 ZONE 06" gives 47 and 6), and it does not read "Div-128" or "W06" at all (171 titles).
- 4,140 titles (58%) carry no ward marker. 1,767 of them contain a name the OpenStreetMap gazetteer holds for their State, but of 30 such titles read by a person only 4 named the locality of the work; 11 were another place of that name, a road's name, a person or a common word (section 8).
- The locality fallback is built: data/wards/india-localities.json, 305,906 named places, 9,108,437 bytes, ODbL. It is thin exactly where the notices are: 5,242 places for Uttar Pradesh against 36,700 for Andhra Pradesh (section 9).

What this decides for the runtime:

1. Outside Bengaluru a ward number is usable only with the body and only where the snapshot's delimitation is the tenders'. Each snapshot's `use` field says which it is: `by: number` for Bhopal, `by: name` for Kanpur, Hyderabad, Delhi and Ahmedabad, `by: nothing` for Jaipur and Chandigarh (and, since the reading of section 11, for Chennai, Coimbatore, Faridabad and Navi Mumbai).
2. A zone, circle or borough number must not be read as a ward. All 12 wrong Bhopal pairs and 10 of Hyderabad's 15 are this.
3. A gazetteer is safe only the other way round: start from the pothole's coordinates, take the handful of places within a kilometre or two, and look for those few names in the titles of that body.

## 10. What "all of India" can and cannot mean

**It can mean** 26 bodies in 16 States and UTs today: a pothole's coordinate becomes a ward, from files anyone may copy, in Ahmedabad, Bhopal, Chennai, Bhubaneswar, Coimbatore, Lucknow, Mumbai, Kolkata, Pune and the rest of section 6 (section 11 found four of those files, Chennai, Coimbatore, Faridabad and Navi Mumbai, numbered for wards that have since been redrawn). For the tenders the app holds now, that reaches 304 of 7,173 notices, and a tested, usable match in two bodies: Ahmedabad by ward name and Bhopal by ward number.

**It cannot mean, with data that exists and may be copied:**

- The bodies that tender the most. Ghaziabad (321 notices), Shamli, Ramkola, Bettiah, Gwalior, Soro, Karimnagar, Srinagar and Alwar have no ward polygons under any licence that allows copying. For most of them the only map found is Esri India's, which forbids export.
- Kerala. 1,378 notices, 862 with a ward number, almost all from grama panchayats. The 2025 ward map exists and is public to look at, not to download.
- Uttar Pradesh beyond Kanpur and Lucknow: 1,664 notices, 833 with a ward number, from 72 urban bodies, and ward files for two of them (one of which is half drawn and numbered for an older delimitation).
- Current wards in the cities that were re-delimited after their open file was drawn: Delhi (2022), Chandigarh (2021), Jaipur (2020), Hyderabad (2026), Kochi (2025), Pune's named file (2025). Their official current layers were opened and counted; none carries a reuse licence.

**What would change that, in order of notices gained:**

1. A licence, or the owner's decision to treat a public official service as usable, for the official layers that were opened and counted: Delhi GSDL (250 wards), TGRAC (155 Hyderabad wards, 885 municipal wards) and Pune's own prabhags, which are in the registry behind `--allow-unlicensed`, and the Mumbai and Chennai corporations' own ward layers, which are not. The Kolkata and Pune outlines in the routing packs already rest on that kind of source (the West Bengal pack says so itself: "no explicit reuse licence was published").
2. Someone fetching the four Government Open Data License files in a browser (Jabalpur has 25 notices, Agra 5) and running the registry command.
3. Asking Ghaziabad Nagar Nigam, Kerala's Delimitation Commission and the Uttar Pradesh Directorate of Local Bodies for their ward layers. No public download of any of them was found.

Until then, the honest product outside these bodies is the one the titles support without any polygon: the body that owns the point (from the tendering office) and the ward number the citizen can read off a property tax bill, matched to "Ward No. N" in that body's titles.

### What the reading rests on

A pair matched by name is right when the title says that ward on its own ward marker. A pair matched by number is right only where the file's ward of that number is shown to be the title's: by a place the title names found inside the ward in OpenStreetMap, by wards one title groups being neighbours in the file, or by a newer ward map. The zone alone is not enough: every Chennai title's zone agreed with the file's, and 8 of its 17 pairs were wrong, because the 200 wards were renumbered inside their zones in 2018.
